# Process models

These are low-cost engineering relationships for reproducible dataset generation, not high-fidelity plant or safety models. Each asset has load, actuator, temperature, and vibration states. Regime load is an input to coupled equations; noise perturbs sensor/process values and never replaces the process equations.

- CNC: load drives spindle speed, power, feed, coolant pressure, vibration, and thermal target; temperature follows a first-order lag.
- Pump generic profile: RPM drives flow; a simple system curve relates RPM/load to pressure; load and pressure drive current; motor temperature lags its target.
- Compressor: load drives current, pressure, discharge/oil temperatures, and vibration.
- Conveyor: load drives current, speed, temperature, vibration, and photoeye rate.

The compressor-only `air_leak` anomaly represents a pressure loss with a compensating increase in motor current. This is a deliberately reduced-order effect inspired by maintenance-reported MetroPT-3 air leaks; it does not simulate pneumatic demand, reservoir dynamics or the original controller. Its loss/gain fractions and progression are sampled from the versioned training or challenge suite, not fitted failure magnitudes. Source and licensing analysis is recorded in [DATASET_PATTERN_REVIEW.md](DATASET_PATTERN_REVIEW.md).

## Versioned compressor profiles

`AssetSpec.process_profile` selects a model independently of the anomaly or evaluator. `generic` is the default and uses the illustrative generic compressor equations. `metropt3_rail_apu` opts into a profile informed by the aggregate MetroPT-3 report in `calibration/metropt-3-summary.json`; it is valid only for compressor assets. Each asset records `process_profile_version` and may override explicit `process_parameters` in scenario YAML. The initial profile release is `1.0.0`; `1.1.0` separates `OFF` stopped current from `IDLE` offloaded current. Version `1.0.0` retains its prior mode behavior for replay compatibility. Supported overrides include loaded/off current and pressure anchors, load and thermal time constants, and sensor-noise scales. Unknown or version-incompatible parameters, non-finite values and non-positive time constants are rejected. A resolved scenario preserves overrides for replay and dataset provenance. See [the v1.1 example](scenarios/normal-compressor-metropt-rail-apu-v1.1.yaml).

The MetroPT profile's mode-conditioned current and pressure envelopes are descriptive. The UCI variable description reports typical motor current near 0 A when stopped, 4 A offloaded, 7 A under load, and 9 A during startup. Profile v1.1 maps `OFF` to stopped, `IDLE` to offloaded current, and samples one approximately 9 A startup value when entering `WARMUP` or a loaded regime from an inactive regime. The source's approximately 10-second cadence cannot determine that pulse's duration, so the one-sample duration is an explicit approximation. These mappings are configurable and do not reconstruct the train controller's digital valve states. The load mapping, thermal time constant, discharge-temperature and vibration equations remain simulator assumptions; this profile is not a digital twin.

The optional pump `centrifugal_vfd` profile v1.0.0 preserves the original fixed-duty affinity model: flow is proportional to speed, pressure/head to speed squared, and hydraulic power to speed cubed; current is estimated from three-phase electrical power. Version 1.1.0 adds a configurable system static-head fraction and dimensionless pump shutoff-head ratio. It intersects a quadratic pump curve through the rated duty point and shutoff point with a quadratic system curve. With zero static head this reduces to the v1.0.0 affinity relationships; with static head, flow no longer scales linearly with speed and the pump can reach a no-flow region below its modeled head threshold. Version 1.0.0 keeps its previous equations for replay compatibility. `training-v0.5.yaml` and `challenge-v0.4.yaml` sample the two curve-shape parameters from explicit synthetic ranges; those distributions are design choices, not field-population estimates.

The shutoff-head ratio, constant efficiency, hydraulic power estimate, thermal target and vibration relation remain simplifying assumptions; they are not a pump-specific curve fit or calibrated digital twin. DOE's variable-speed pumping guide notes that affinity-law approximations may be substantially inaccurate in systems with high static head, motivating the explicit v1.1 curve parameters rather than treating affinity laws as universally exact. No source dataset was copied into the simulator. The AI4EU robotic paint-pump record may support a separately scoped future transient study, but its equipment differs from the centrifugal water-pump profile and its lower-rate channels are interpolated. A candidate cavitation dataset is excluded because its metadata and README disagree on license. See the [source review](docs/workflows/synthetic-dataset-generation/runs/2026-10-04-ai4eu-pump-source-review.md), the [`normal-pump-centrifugal-vfd.yaml`](scenarios/normal-pump-centrifugal-vfd.yaml) example, and the official [DOE guide](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2014/05/f16/variable_speed_pumping.pdf).

Versioned suite distributions may use `generation.asset_process_profiles` to select a process profile independently for each asset of the declared classes. Profile parameters accept literal values, numeric ranges, or choices and are resolved from each run seed before validation. `training-v0.4.yaml` and `challenge-v0.3.yaml` retain pump profile v1.0.0; `training-v0.5.yaml` and `challenge-v0.4.yaml` sample v1.1.0 static-head and shutoff-head parameters. Old suites preserve their prior outputs and process-model defaults. Training manifests include each run's process profile/version and aggregate counts. Hidden challenge outputs omit the resolved scenario and profile details.

Generic process assets support reproducible per-asset overrides for `load_scale`, `actuator_tau_s`, `thermal_time_constant_scale`, `sensor_noise_scale`, and initial temperature offset. The normal-operation suite samples these in declared ranges so otherwise normal runs include distinct production setpoints, response speeds, initial thermal states, and sensor variability. The numeric ranges are a benchmark design choice, not inferred industrial population distributions.

OFF, IDLE, WARMUP, LOW_LOAD, NORMAL_LOAD, HIGH_LOAD, COOLDOWN, and MAINTENANCE are scenario regime options. The baseline schedule has startup, load transitions/recipe changes, and shutdown. Add validated shift schedules and environmental profiles in the next phase.
